## What the "blogging is dead" argument actually proves

Strip the doom out of it and the argument is narrower than it sounds. It says: text is now cheap to generate, so text no longer signals effort, so text no longer carries value.

The first two parts are true. The third does not follow.

What became worthless is *undifferentiated* writing — the SEO-shaped, keyword-first, could-have-been-written-by-anyone kind. That was already a commodity before the models arrived; they just made it obvious. What did not become worthless is a specific person with a specific point of view, real experience, and something at stake in the argument.

If anything, when the volume of generic text goes up, the value of a voice you recognise goes up with it. Scarcity moved. It did not disappear.

---

## So why does it feel like it is over?

Because two things broke at the same time, and neither of them is about the quality of your writing.

### 1. Short-form video is where people get information, and bloggers aren't there

Most people now discover ideas through video. That is where the attention is, and it is not going back.

Bloggers have almost no presence on those platforms — not because their ideas do not work in video, but because video production is a completely different skill set with a completely different cost structure. Writing a good 1,500-word post and producing a good 60-second video have almost nothing in common operationally. So writers keep publishing into the channel they know, while the audience keeps moving to the one they do not.

That is a distribution gap, not a talent gap.

### 2. Social feeds got worse at surfacing human writing

The second problem is discovery. Social platforms are now saturated with AI-generated content, which makes it materially harder to find authentic human writing in the flood.

And the algorithms are not helping, because they are not optimising for what you would want them to. They optimise for engagement, which in practice means clickbait — not insight, not craft, not the piece that changes how someone thinks.

So the writer with the genuinely good essay is competing against an infinite supply of content engineered specifically to beat the ranking system. Being good is not the constraint. Being *found* is.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is blogging actually dead in 2026?**
No. Generic, undifferentiated writing lost its value because it became free to produce. Writing with a recognisable voice, real experience, and an actual point of view did not — it got scarcer relative to everything else.

**If AI text is indistinguishable from human text, does authenticity even matter?**
It matters at the level of the body of work, not the individual paragraph. Readers may not be able to classify a single sentence, but they can tell over time whether a writer has been anywhere, believes anything, or is worth returning to.

**Why do bloggers need video at all?**
Because that is where the audience discovers ideas now. The point is not to abandon writing for video — it is to stop letting the format gap decide who gets found.

**Does turning a blog post into a video make it AI slop?**
It depends entirely on what is being generated. If the model writes the ideas, you get slop. If the model handles the production work and your writing supplies the substance, you get your own piece in a second format.

**What is bloghub.app?**
A launch platform for publications — Product Hunt for writing. It is in beta, built to give new writers a discovery surface that is not a social algorithm optimising for clickbait.
