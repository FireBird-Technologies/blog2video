# How to Get a YouTube Backlink (DR 99) in Minutes, Without Filming Anything

> **TL;DR:** Ahrefs rates youtube.com at DR 99. Every video you upload gets a permanent, indexed watch page on that domain — carrying your title, your description, your link, and your transcript. The description link is nofollow, so this is not a PageRank injection; it is a Google-owned page that ranks for your topic and points at you. The blocker was always production time. [Blog2Video](https://blog2video.app) renders a video from a post in about two minutes, in code rather than a diffusion model, in your own voice.

## The highest-authority page you are allowed to publish on

There is no outreach involved. No guest post pitch, no HARO reply, no paying $200 to a link vendor whose "editorial placements" are all on the same five expired domains.

You upload a video. YouTube gives you a watch page on a domain Ahrefs scores at **DR 99** — the same domain Google owns, indexes constantly, and surfaces in its own results with a thumbnail attached. That page carries your title, up to 5,000 characters of description, your link, your transcript, and your channel name. It stays up indefinitely.

Google is not neutral about its own product. Video results get pulled into normal search results, into the Videos tab, and increasingly into AI answers that cite what they watched. A page on youtube.com is not a link on a blog. It is a second surface.

## The honest part: the description link is nofollow

Let us get this out of the way, because you will read "DR 99 backlink" on Twitter about once a week and it is never qualified.

Links in a YouTube description carry `rel="nofollow"`. They always have. Since March 2020 Google has treated nofollow as a *hint* rather than a hard directive — it may consider the link for crawling and ranking — but nobody should plan on link equity flowing from a video description into their domain. If your entire strategy is "get the DR 99 juice," you will be disappointed.

Here is what you actually get, and why it is still worth two minutes:

- **A ranking page you control on someone else's authority.** Your video can rank for your topic on Google and inside YouTube's own search, which is the second-largest search engine in the world.
- **Referral traffic that converts.** Someone who watched ninety seconds of your explanation and then clicked your link is a warmer visitor than anything a directory sends you.
- **Brand and entity signals.** Repeated co-occurrence of your brand name, your topic, and your URL across a high-trust domain is exactly the association graph that both Google and LLM answer engines build from.
- **A discovery surface that is not a feed.** It keeps working after you stop promoting it, which is more than LinkedIn or Notes will do for you.

Call it what it is: a free, permanent, indexed page on the strongest domain you will ever be handed. The nofollow attribute does not make that worthless — it just makes it a different asset than the one people advertise.

## So why does almost nobody collect it?

Because making the video costs more than the link is worth.

A talking-head take means a camera, a room that does not echo, three attempts, and an hour in an editor. Nobody does that for a blog post they already published. So the video never gets made, and the DR 99 page never exists.

The obvious escape hatch — generic AI video generators — has turned out to be a trap. Prompt-to-video output has a look now, and viewers have learned it: the drifting camera, the melting hands, the stock-corporate voice reading someone else's copy. Those videos do not get watched. A video with eleven views does not rank, does not get cited, and does not send anybody to your site. You end up with a technically-real backlink on a page nobody has ever loaded.

## Uploading so the page actually earns something

The video is the easy half. The upload is where most of the SEO value is won or lost.

- **Put the link in the first line of the description.** Only the first two lines show above the fold. A URL buried under 400 words of hashtags gets clicked by nobody.
- **Write a real description, not a keyword pile.** YouTube indexes it, Google reads it, and answer engines quote it. Two to three hundred words summarising the post, with your link at the top and again at the bottom.
- **Upload subtitles.** YouTube will auto-caption you, but auto-captions mangle product names and technical terms — precisely the words you want indexed. You already have the script, so export it as an SRT and upload it.
- **Paste the transcript or a summary into the description.** More indexable text on a DR 99 page, describing your topic, in your words.
- **Title it for search, not for cleverness.** The video should target the same keyword as the post, phrased the way someone would actually type it.
- **Pin a comment with the link.** It survives description truncation and is the second-most-clicked element on the page.
- **Add chapters and an end screen.** Chapters get pulled into Google's key-moments display, and the end screen catches viewers who made it to the end.
